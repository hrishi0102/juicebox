# How AI was used

ETHGlobal asks every team to document where and how AI tools were used. This is that record.

## Summary

The first working version (V1) of Receipts was generated by **Claude Code** (model Claude Opus 5.5) overnight on Nov 6, 2026, from the written brief in [brief.md](brief.md). The human team member wrote the idea and the brief, chose the direction, and reviews, tests, changes and demos the result during the hackathon.

## What the AI did

- Researched the ETHGlobal Mumbai prize pages and rules, and the 0G, Chainlink CRE, Arc, Arkiv and EF CROPS docs, then recommended which three partner tracks to enter.
- Studied the structure of the local Claude Code logs (record types and fields only) to design the parser.
- Wrote the code in:
  - `web/src/lib/receipts/` (parser, pricing, GitHub client, matching, outcomes, judge, scoring, receipts, Arkiv, demo data)
  - `web/src/app/` and `web/src/components/` (pages, API routes, UI components other than `components/ui`)
  - `web/scripts/receipts.ts` (CLI)
  - `cre/receipts-monthly/` (Chainlink CRE workflow) and `contracts/` (TeamScoreboard, tests, deploy script)
  - `README.md`, `arkiv/*.md`, this file
  - `.githooks/pre-commit` (blocks transcripts from being committed)
- Ran the pipeline on the author's own logs to check accuracy (prices against Claude Code's own tracker, links to real pull requests) and fixed the rules based on what it found.

## Day 2 (Nov 6): the product change

After reviewing V1, the human team member changed the direction: the front door became "What did you get done this week?" (a fun, shareable list, not a cost dashboard), PRs stopped being the only measure, nothing may run in the background, one login (GitHub), PR lists always shared with the company, and the card is a download with cost hidden by default. Claude Code then built, from those decisions:

- The two-call private AI on 0G (per session, then per week), its prompts and parsers (`judge-core.ts`, `judge.ts`, `week.ts`, `story.ts`), live-tested on the author's logs.
- The new home page, the share studio and card (`app/share`, `app/api/card`), receipt v2 with item proofs, the laptop signing key, signer-owned read-only Arkiv records, the company and team pages, GitHub identity and sign-in, and Sign in with 0G.
- The architecture docs, README, Arkiv docs and this submission kit.
- AI Wrapped (`/wrapped`), from the human team member's request for a general, non-coding Wrapped with fun segments (overthinking, AI as a therapist, just saying hi): the counting rules, persona, page, card, ChatGPT and Claude.ai export reader, and the optional roast on 0G. The rules were tuned on counts from the author's own logs; no messages were committed.

The human team member supplied the 0G key, made every product call above, and does the sign-ins, key setup, demo video and submission.

## Day 3 (Nov 7): one flow per role

The human team member found the app confusing: every role (employee, company, public, demo, Wrapped) and every sign-in (GitHub, 0G, the laptop key) shared one header and one home page. They set the direction: the company product is the main one (employees send signed receipts, the company sees everyone's), AI Wrapped is a separate side product, each kind of user gets its own flow shown on its own, keep only one way to pay for the AI, and keep Sign in with 0G because the 0G bonus rewards it. Claude Code proposed the split (a diagram of the two sides and the side product), answered where GitHub, 0G and the receipt-versus-card line belong, then built it:

- A front door; separate parts with their own layouts, headers, tabs and identity: My week (`app/me`: setup, this week, review, sign, send), Company (`app/company`: overview, teams, people, pull requests, setup), For fun (`app/(personal)`), and public pages; redirects from the old addresses.
- Sign in with 0G as the one way to pay, shared across the app (`components/zero-g.tsx`, `lib/zerog-tasks.ts`), with the balance shown before and after; the review and roast routes moved to the same browser-pays path; the app-key web routes removed.
- Joining a company from your GitHub teams (`/api/join`), lists opened by org membership (`lib/server/viewer.ts`), keeping single lines private when sending (`/api/share`), the `/demo` role picker and banner, and the company setup checklist.
- Tests for the weekly steps and for sending with lines kept private; docs and decisions 41 to 46.
- At the human team member's request, a check of every Arkiv and Swarm prize requirement against the repo and live data: the documented queries and links were run (read-only), the README got a runnable query (Arkiv refuses anonymous queries), the judging files were brought up to date, the company page now keeps the latest of a week sent twice by `$createdAt`, and the public receipt page shows read-only, owner, writer and expiry from Arkiv.
- At the human team member's request, a GitHub key proof (a public gist signed by the laptop key) was built and then removed the same day when they found it unclear (decisions 47 and 49).

After testing the whole flow themselves, the human team member asked for these changes, and Claude Code made them:

- Sending without picking or editing: the Send page shows the whole receipt with two steps, sign and send; the keep-private switch and the review step are gone (decision 48).
- For fun as one page: My AI Wrapped with Share, Post on X, Download and Copy; week cards, the wall and their Swarm and Arkiv code removed, with redirects (decision 50).
- "Check it yourself" links wherever the app claims something happened on 0G, Arkiv or Swarm, and two live proof pages: `/proof/swarm/<ref>` (fetches the ciphertext, so the Swarm link no longer just downloads a file) and `/proof/0g/<provider>` (0G's TEE verdicts) (decision 51).
- The 0G balance read in neuron and shown in 0G, with what each call cost (decision 52), and a sidebar for the company and employee pages (decision 53).
- Tests for the 0G balance and for sending the whole week sealed on Swarm; docs brought up to date.
- At the human team member's request, a ranking of the other prize tracks by how naturally they fit (they dropped the Ethereum Foundation track), then the Chainlink CRE job brought up to date for the chosen track: the check moved to `cre/receipts-monthly/score.ts` with signature checks inside the workflow, a Node runner against live Arkiv data, the WASM build, the `TeamScoreboard` contract updated, tested and deployed on Sepolia, and the company's Team page reading the score (decision 54).
