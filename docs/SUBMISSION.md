## Submission text

**Project name:** Receipts

**Short description (one line):** What did your AI spend get done? Each employee's laptop has a private AI on 0G, paid from their own 0G credits, write a signed weekly receipt from their Claude Code and Codex sessions; the company sees everyone's on Arkiv.

**Description:**
AI tools make it easy to spend a lot and hard to say what came out of it, and token leaderboards reward burning tokens, not getting things done. Receipts gives a company the answer, one signed weekly receipt per person. Each employee runs it on their laptop: it reads their Claude Code and Codex logs, links sessions to the pull requests they produced, and, when they click Write my week, has a private AI on the 0G Private Computer, running inside a hardware enclave (TEE), read each session and write one line: Built, Fixed, Figured out, Researched, Wrote, Planned or Wasted, with a short title. They sign in with their own 0G account, so it is paid from their own daily credits. They sign the whole week with a key made on their laptop (nothing can be left out or edited) and send it. The company, which is just the GitHub org, signs in with GitHub and sees everyone's signed list, the PRs behind it and the totals by category and AI tool. Never prompts. As a separate side product, anyone can see their AI Wrapped and share it: how much time they spent overthinking, treating the AI as a therapist or just saying hi, counted with simple rules from their logs or a ChatGPT or Claude.ai export that never leaves the browser.

**How it's made:**
Next.js 16 and TypeScript with shadcn/ui, running locally. Streaming readers for Claude Code (with subagents) and Codex logs price every call and match Claude Code's own cost tracker within 2%. Sessions are linked to PRs through the PR links and git operations in the logs, branches and file overlap. The private AI calls the 0G router in private trust mode (TEE-only), model `0gm-1.0-35b-a3b-sia` with thinking off (about 2 seconds and $0.001 a call), falling back to `glm-5.3`; the router verifies each TEE signature and we re-check it with the 0G SDK. The weekly call sees only the titles, never prompts. Every private AI call in the web app is paid with Sign in with 0G: 0G's Privy app and the router's `/v1/auth/privy` session, so each person pays from their own credits (the page shows the balance in 0G and what each call cost) while the laptop's server keeps building every message. The Wrapped card is drawn with `next/og`. Receipts are EIP-712 typed data with three Merkle roots (the items, the PRs and the 0G calls), so any edit breaks the signature and a totals-only copy still checks against it. Sending is one Arkiv transaction that creates a read-only entity and hands its ownership to the signer; the company side queries Arkiv by org, re-verifies every signature, and opens the sealed lists for members of the GitHub org. Every claim links to where it can be checked: the Arkiv explorer, a page that fetches the Swarm copy live, and 0G's TEE verdicts read live from the router.

**0G (Build with 0G Private Computer):**
The private AI is the product's core and must read prompts and the AI's answers to say what you got. Every call goes through the 0G router with `X-0G-Provider-Trust-Mode: private` and `verify_tee`, and we re-check the TEE signature ourselves. Each result keeps its request id, provider, TEE checks, cost, the paying 0G wallet and hashes of what was sent and answered, which go into the signed receipt. iAI: signing in with your 0G account is part of the employee's setup, and it is the only way the web app pays: Write my week, the private review and Roast me all spend the person's own daily credits, and the page shows their balance in 0G and what each call cost. `/proof/0g/<provider>` reads 0G's TEE verdicts for the provider that ran the calls live from the router, with links to ChainScan and pc.0g.ai. Why someone comes back tomorrow: a new receipt every week. What breaks without 0G: the AI that writes the list; we refuse to send prompts to an ordinary API, so it falls back to rules. Code: `web/src/lib/receipts/judge-core.ts`, `judge.ts`, `week.ts`, `web/src/components/zero-g.tsx`, `web/src/lib/zerog-balance.ts`, `web/src/lib/zerog-tasks.ts`, `web/src/app/(public)/proof/0g`.

**Arkiv (Build on Arkiv, from scratch, + the Arkiv + Swarm bonus):**
Shared weeks are Arkiv entities (schema `receipts-ethglobal-mumbai.receipt.v2`, Rabdi testnet) with indexed org, team, week, signer, GitHub login and numbers. One `executeBatch` creates each entity read-only and hands ownership to the signer, so the company pays gas but the employee owns an uneditable record. With Swarm, the full week is encrypted on the laptop and stored on Swarm; the Arkiv record keeps only the signed totals, the Swarm reference, and the key sealed to the company, so everyone can verify the numbers while only the company can open the list. The company side reads Arkiv directly and re-checks signatures, and keeps the latest receipt per person and week by `$createdAt`. A shared receipt's page shows, straight from Arkiv, that it is read-only, owned by the person who signed it, written by a wallet that only paid the fee, and when it expires. Every page that shows a receipt links to it on the Arkiv explorer (the record, its transaction, its owner), and `/proof/swarm/<ref>` fetches the Swarm copy live and, for company members, checks it against the SHA-256 sealed in the record. See `/arkiv` for schema, usage, why Arkiv, reach, friction, and `arkiv/swarm.md` for the Swarm details.

**Chainlink (Best Workflow with CRE):**
Receipts are written by employees and read by their company, and both sides have a reason to bend the numbers. Once a month a CRE workflow checks each team on its own: every node reads the team's receipts straight from Arkiv (one `arkiv_query` JSON-RPC call), checks each receipt's EIP-712 signature against the person it is about (recomputed in the workflow, so edited receipts are dropped), adds up the signed numbers with one receipt per person per week, and asks the GitHub API whether each PR a receipt claims as merged is still merged. Consensus takes the median of every number, and the report is written through Chainlink's forwarder to our `TeamScoreboard` contract on Ethereum Sepolia (`0x563F8f8A06a9bCb394Bd463CEC352e6EB902FDD5`), which only the forwarder can post to and which never lets a month be rewritten. The company's Team page reads the latest score from the contract and links to the transaction. One chain, two outside systems (Arkiv and GitHub), and the workflow is what makes the score trustworthy without our server or the company's computer. Code: `cre/receipts-monthly/score.ts`, `main.ts`, `contracts/src/TeamScoreboard.sol`, `web/src/lib/server/scoreboard.ts`.
