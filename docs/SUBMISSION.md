## Submission text

**Project name:** Receipts

**Short description (one line):** What did your AI spend get done? Each employee's laptop has a private AI on 0G, paid from their own 0G credits, write a signed weekly receipt from their Claude Code and Codex sessions; the company sees everyone's on Arkiv.

**Description:**
AI tools make it easy to spend a lot and hard to say what came out of it, and token leaderboards reward burning tokens, not getting things done. Receipts gives a company the answer, one signed weekly receipt per person. Each employee runs it on their laptop: it reads their Claude Code and Codex logs, links sessions to the pull requests they produced, and, when they click Write my week, has a private AI on the 0G Private Computer, running inside a hardware enclave (TEE), read each session and write one line: Built, Fixed, Figured out, Researched, Wrote, Planned or Wasted, with a short title. They sign in with their own 0G account, so it is paid from their own daily credits. They review exactly what the company will see (and can keep single lines private), sign it with a key made on their laptop, and send it. The company, which is just the GitHub org, signs in with GitHub and sees everyone's signed list, the PRs behind it and the totals by category and AI tool. Never prompts. As a separate side product, anyone can post their week as a card or see their AI Wrapped: how much time they spent overthinking, treating the AI as a therapist or just saying hi, counted with simple rules from their logs or a ChatGPT or Claude.ai export that never leaves the browser.

**How it's made:**
Next.js 16 and TypeScript with shadcn/ui, running locally. Streaming readers for Claude Code (with subagents) and Codex logs price every call and match Claude Code's own cost tracker within 2%. Sessions are linked to PRs through the PR links and git operations in the logs, branches and file overlap. The private AI calls the 0G router in private trust mode (TEE-only), model `0gm-1.0-35b-a3b-sia` with thinking off (about 2 seconds and $0.001 a call), falling back to `glm-5.3`; the router verifies each TEE signature and we re-check it with the 0G SDK. The weekly call sees only the titles, never prompts. Every private AI call in the web app is paid with Sign in with 0G: 0G's Privy app and the router's `/v1/auth/privy` session, so each person pays from their own credits (the page shows the balance before and after) while the laptop's server keeps building every message. The week card is drawn with `next/og`. Receipts are EIP-712 typed data with three Merkle roots (the items, the PRs and the 0G calls), so an owner can hide one item with proofs while the signed totals stay intact, and any edit breaks the signature. Sending is one Arkiv transaction that creates a read-only entity and hands its ownership to the signer; the company side queries Arkiv by org, re-verifies every signature, and opens the sealed lists for members of the GitHub org.

**0G (Build with 0G Private Computer):**
The private AI is the product's core and must read prompts and the AI's answers to say what you got. Every call goes through the 0G router with `X-0G-Provider-Trust-Mode: private` and `verify_tee`, and we re-check the TEE signature ourselves. Each result keeps its request id, provider, TEE checks, cost, the paying 0G wallet and hashes of what was sent and answered, which go into the signed receipt. iAI: signing in with your 0G account is part of the employee's setup, and it is the only way the web app pays: Write my week, the private review and Roast me all spend the person's own daily credits, and the page shows the balance going down. Why someone comes back tomorrow: a new receipt every week. What breaks without 0G: the AI that writes the list; we refuse to send prompts to an ordinary API, so it falls back to rules. Code: `web/src/lib/receipts/judge-core.ts`, `judge.ts`, `week.ts`, `web/src/components/zero-g.tsx`, `web/src/lib/zerog-tasks.ts`.

**Arkiv (Build on Arkiv + Swarm bonus):**
Shared weeks are Arkiv entities (schema `receipts-ethglobal-mumbai.receipt.v2`, Rabdi testnet) with indexed org, team, week, signer, GitHub login and numbers. One `executeBatch` creates each entity read-only and hands ownership to the signer, so the company pays gas but the employee owns an uneditable record. With Swarm, the full week is encrypted on the laptop and stored on Swarm; the Arkiv record keeps only the signed totals, the Swarm reference, and the key sealed to the company, so everyone can verify the numbers while only the company can open the list. Public cards are stored on Swarm and listed on Arkiv (`card.v1`), shown on the wall. The company side reads Arkiv directly and re-checks signatures. See `/arkiv` for schema, usage, why Arkiv, reach, friction, and `arkiv/swarm.md` for the Swarm details.

**Ethereum Foundation (Best CROPS App):**
Lead property: Private. Raw logs never leave the laptop; only a trimmed digest of each session goes to 0G's private computer (model in a TEE), and only when you ask; there is no Receipts server; weeks are signed with a key that stays on the laptop and stored on Arkiv as records the person owns; the week card is drawn locally; no trackers. The README has a CROPS section with what each third party learns and our trust assumptions.
